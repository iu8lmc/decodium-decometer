Decometer brings the RF meter of Decodium 4 to your phone: forward power, SWR,
ALC and power-amplifier telemetry, read from the PC over your home network.

It matters when Decodium holds the radio. No other program can open the same
serial port, and whoever operates from the next room — or from the other side
of the world — used to transmit without seeing how much power was going out,
or whether the antenna was answering at all. This app opens that window.

THE METER FACE
It is the same instrument as on the computer, not a reduced version: same
geometry, same colours, same formulas, same needle ballistics. If you know it
on the desktop, you find it identical in your hand.

Readings are never invented. When the radio does not provide a meter, two
dashes appear and the status line says why. An instrument showing a plausible
but false number is worse than one that stays silent — which is also why, with
the transmitter at rest, the transmit meters read nothing instead of zero: a
zero would say "no power, perfect SWR", which looks like a station working
beautifully.

FIVE PAGES ON THE SAME FACE
Power (forward, reflected, SWR), matching (return loss, mismatch loss, net
power), drive (ALC, PEP, average), signal (dBm, S-meter, frequency and band),
and the power amplifier: drain voltage, drain current, temperature, speech
compression and the position of the power knob, for the rigs that report them.
HOLD freezes what you see — useful on a phone, where you look at the meter
after releasing the PTT.

THE ANTENNA ANALYSER
It collects frequency-and-SWR pairs while you operate and builds the curve of
your antenna, band by band: resonant frequency, bandwidth, and above all the
reactance with its sign — which tells you whether the antenna wants shortening
or lengthening.

For resistance it reports two values instead of one, and that is not
indecision: from SWR alone the two possibilities produce identical curves, and
picking one would be inventing. The Smith chart draws the circle of what was
measured and the arcs of what is compatible with it.

There is also a sweep that commands the radio and transmits, to measure faster.
It starts only if you start it, stops whenever you want, and puts the radio
back where it was.

DECODES
The UDP traffic Decodium already emits, sorted by mode: FT8, FT4, FT2 and the
others, with signal report, time offset and frequency. The counters at the top
double as filters, to watch one mode at a time.

DX CLUSTER SPOTS
The spots your PC is receiving, filterable by band. Not a second telnet session
to the node: the very same spots the operator has in front of them.

SWR ALARM
Choose a threshold and the phone vibrates when the SWR goes past it while you
transmit. It is the bench wattmeter's beep, translated for something that sits
in your pocket: an alarm you can only see is no alarm when you are in another
room.

HOW IT CONNECTS
On the PC, in Decodium 4, turn on the DecoPort gateway and choose a password.
The computer announces itself on the network: in the app the radio appears in a
list, you tap it, type the same password, and from then on it reconnects on its
own at every start. There is no IP address to remember.

The link is authenticated and there is no cleartext mode: without the password
it does not connect at all, which on a port that exposes a radio is not a
detail. Nothing goes through the internet and nothing needs to be exposed
outside.

WHAT IT DOES NOT DO
It does not decode, does not record audio, does not touch the serial port, does
not ask for the microphone. The only permission it needs is local network
access. The only thing that commands the radio is the analyser's sweep, and
that runs only when you ask for it.

REQUIREMENTS
Decodium 4 with the DecoPort gateway, running on the PC, and the phone on the
same WiFi network.
